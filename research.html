Research

I have been undertaking a project that tries to let a neural-network-based model learn and make prediction of BCJ numerators. BCJ numerators are an important approach for gravitational amplitudes, while people have not found universal construction of them in many theories and not even certain about their existence at loop levels. We turn the analytic expressions into sentences made of tokens or other schemes, train a model learn and predict, and we also want to extract useful information which is hidden to people but seen by models. A surprising progress using Transformer was reported in 2024. Furthermore we want to have a framework of such learning.

Recently I switch my gear into conformal field theory and defects, motivated by their role in understanding the dynamics of field theory. And they are also connected to real-world physics, critical phenomena. As a beginner, I'm still trying and exploring different topics, while also enhancing my calculation ability.

Currently I have been reading papers on defect fusion. The fusion of defects was first studied in the 2d interface setup in 2007. Recently people have understood the fusion process as an effective field theory and an RG running with the fusing distance. This motivates studies of the universal Casimir energy, anomalies, cusps, and comparison with OPE, which might provide insights into algebraic structures like (defect) fusion rules and also calculation and bootstrap aspects.

As a side joy, I tried a toy lattice model in an economics setup (each lattice means a person living there) and manipulated it by adding a street in it as the "defect". This model has features like local interaction,. After constructing a global potential (as the action) and computing the transition probablity amplitude (to define partition function), we can perform tricks in statistical field theory!

This also motivates me to think more about RG and critical phenomena, also many-body dynamics.
